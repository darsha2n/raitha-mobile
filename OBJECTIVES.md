# Raitha objectives

Help Kannada- and English-speaking farmers estimate crop profitability and
compare selling options using clearly labelled acres and quintals.

1. Accept the acreage under one crop, including fractional acres.
2. Accept saleable yield in quintals PER ACRE and price PER QUINTAL.
3. Accept each production expense PER ACRE for one crop cycle.
4. Show revenue, expenses and profit separately per acre and for the full crop area.
5. Show full-area harvest in quintals, break-even price per quintal,
   break-even yield per acre and full-area break-even harvest.
6. Compare market proceeds using total harvest in quintals, commission and
   transport cost for the entire shipment.
7. Explain conversions: one quintal is 100 kg. Never silently mix kg and quintals.
8. Preserve English/Kannada support, phone installation and offline calculations.
9. Make all assumptions visible. Estimates assume uniform yield, costs and price
   across the entered crop area. A 12-acre farm with only 4 acres under this
   crop should enter 4 acres, not 12.
10. Keep calculations private: no Google connection or remote record collection.
11. Offer named-variety yield references with growing condition, region,
    crop duration, original units, source links and verification date.
12. Keep source references separate from the farmer's expected saleable yield;
    never assume a university variety potential is guaranteed on this farm.
13. Show hypothetical profit at published yield bounds using entered costs
    and selling price, without presenting these as best/worst forecasts.

## Example: one crop across 12 acres

Yield 20 quintals per acre; price INR 2,200 per quintal;
total of all cost categories INR 14,500 per acre.

| Measure | Per acre | All 12 acres |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Saleable harvest | 20 quintals | 240 quintals |
| Revenue | INR 44,000 | INR 528,000 |
| Expenses | INR 14,500 | INR 174,000 |
| Estimated profit | INR 29,500 | INR 354,000 |

Break-even price is INR 725 per quintal. Break-even yield is approximately
6.59 quintals per acre, or 79.09 quintals for all 12 acres.

This is an illustration, not a confirmation of any farmer's actual inputs.
